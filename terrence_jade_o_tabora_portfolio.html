Problem-Solving & Methodology

Engineering approach to debugging embedded hardware-software constraints and real-world system anomalies.

1. Systematic Debugging

When hardware and software fail to communicate, I isolate variables using serial monitors, oscilloscope diagnostics, and modular unit testing. Every anomaly is treated as a symptom of timing, voltage drop, or logic mismatch.

2. Resource Optimization

Embedded systems have strict constraints on RAM, clock speed, and power. I optimize Python vision pipelines and C++ firmware to run efficiently on low-spec single-board computers and microcontrollers without sacrificing reliability.

3. Fault-Tolerant Design

In IoT and automation builds, sensor noise and connection drops are inevitable. I implement watchdog timers, robust exception handling, and auto-reconnect protocols to ensure autonomous systems recover gracefully from failures.

REFLECT

The Intersection of Software & Hardware

Throughout my BSIT journey at Datamex College of Saint Adeline, I realized that true technological innovation happens at the boundary where lines of code interact with physical reality. Writing software that compiles and executes on a screen is powerful; writing firmware and AI vision algorithms that command motors, interpret sensor streams, and react to physical environments is transformative.

My projects—from offline AI vision systems on Orange Pi to automated recycling machines and Bluetooth e-bike ignitions—have taught me patience, meticulous debugging, and systems thinking. As I step into the professional world as a Software Developer, I am eager to apply this multidisciplinary mindset to build scalable, intelligent, and robust digital solutions.
